## fresh-c-001

Proposed classification: FALSE_POSITIVE.
A negative answer to a negatively phrased supervision question is ambiguous; the affidavit clarifies occasional supervision. The opinion expressly identifies that ambiguity.

Source: Barrera Solano v. A Navas Party Production, Inc., Doc. 64 (S.D. Fla. July 26, 2010)
URL: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-flsd-1_09-cv-22847/pdf/USCOURTS-flsd-1_09-cv-22847-0.pdf

Quote 1: Q. Is it true that Francisco did not supervise you? A. No.

Quote 2: Francisco Navarette [sic] would give me orders approximately once a week.

Court ruling: Because the Plaintiff’s prior testimony was not “clear” the Court may properly consider the Affidavit at the summary judgment stage; whether Plaintiff’s testimony is credible is for the jury to decide.

## fresh-c-002

Proposed classification: FALSE_POSITIVE.
General knowledge that bulbs get hot is compatible with lacking knowledge about a specific indirect countertop ignition hazard; court calls the questions general and affidavit more specific.

Source: Dockhorn v. Kitchens by Kleweno, Doc. 195 (D. Kan. Mar. 23, 2010)
URL: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-ksd-2_08-cv-02307/pdf/USCOURTS-ksd-2_08-cv-02307-4.pdf

Quote 1: Q. Regardless of whether or not you were warned, you knew that halogen lights got hot? A. Correct.

Quote 2: Even though I knew that all light bulbs get hot, I had no reason to know that clothes and bed sheets sitting on the countertop below the halogen lights, but not in direct contact with the lights, would be a fire hazard.

Court ruling: The Court agrees with plaintiff that the deposition testimony and these three paragraphs are not necessarily inconsistent on this point.
