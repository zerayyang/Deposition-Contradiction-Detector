Generally, a party may not file an affidavit that contradicts earlier deposition testimony in an attempt to create a factual issue. Reid v. Sears Roebuck and Co., 790 F.2d 453 (6th Cir. 1986). If a witness, who has knowledge of a fact, is questioned during her deposition about that fact, she is required to “bring it out at the deposition and cannot contradict her testimony in a subsequent affidavit.” Id. The court agrees that Wanda Maynard’s affidavits, filed after her deposition, directly contradict her deposition testimony and “constitute an attempt to create a sham fact issue.” See Areal, S.R.L. v.
