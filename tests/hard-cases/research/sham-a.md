## Exactness, provenance, and detector formatting

For all 13 PDF-based cases, an author-only local script asserted that each `quote1`, `quote2`, and one-sentence `rulingQuote` is a contiguous substring of the cited PDF page extraction after **whitespace-only collapse**. Words, punctuation, brackets, ellipses, spelling, qualifications, and negation are preserved. Each record contains SHA256 of the exact downloaded PDF, page references for both quotes and ruling, court/year, reconstructed-question flags, and proposed status. Eight representative cited pages were rendered and visually inspected: Crawford18, West6, Maisha4, Kabba3, O’Brien46, Goldenson125, Dins4, Dins5. All source page numbers were checked against PDF page extraction and visible printed/page-header markers. Historical HTML cases are transparently marked with no source hash and location limitations.

The opinion itself occasionally uses brackets, ellipses, or quoted fragments. These remain literal in the proposed detector text; they are not represented as complete unabridged original depositions. Examples: Maisha’s editorial pronouns; West’s lower-case “never…” fragment; Crawford’s ellipsis in the jurisdictions fragment; Kabba’s bracketed first letter/period. The full original underlying deposition and affidavit exhibits were not obtained. Their source descriptions and pinpoints are copied from the opinions, not independently authenticated docket exhibits.

Actual examiner questions remain Q and witness answers A. For detector formatting only, Q./A. or Q:/A: role tags are canonicalized to Q:/A: and placed on separate lines; this changes role-tag punctuation and whitespace, **not witness words or punctuation**. Original `quote1` fields retain source role tags. Consequently a whole Q/A quote need not be a byte-identical substring of the formatted detector transcript; removing/canonicalizing role tags recovers exact witness text. Where an opinion quotes only affidavit/declaration prose, a neutral Q is reconstructed and explicitly flagged. Answer text remains unchanged. Kabba’s initial question is reconstructed separately before the verbatim answer/follow-up Q/A; it is not embedded inside a witness answer. Judicial rulings, proposed labels, interpretive explanations, and approval status stay outside both detector transcript fields.

The court’s sham-affidavit disposition is not mechanically mapped to an app category. Maisha’s temporal insurance issue is DEBATABLE: author semantic inference does not establish a judicial INFERENTIAL finding. Junkins is DEBATABLE because the majority’s sham finding and dissent’s necessary-versus-sufficient-condition analysis diverge. Dins is DEBATABLE because qualified present recollection and a later remembered event do not literally conflict, while the court excludes the later event for unexplained supplementation after exhaustive inquiry. O’Brien’s required-versus-instructed pair remains FALSE_POSITIVE on the court’s explicit limited reading; its qualification stays in the notes without marking the label unclear. All and only DEBATABLE labels have `debatable:true`. No deterministic score or detector accuracy is claimed.
