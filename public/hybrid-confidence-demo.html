What happens behind the scenes?

Two model identities each get three versions of the question. That makes six votes. They can make similar mistakes, so we do not treat them as six fully independent experts.

The calibrator is a formula in code. It learns how votes, reviewer objections and evidence checks relate to correct labels using human-approved examples. It is not another AI voting, and the parts do not get fixed percentage shares.

For this pretend demo only, the formula uses these made-up weights: agreement 3; checker agreement 0.5; objection −1; rule support 0.25; rule strength 0.25; starting value −1.

The final step is 100 ÷ (1 + exp(−z)). With five agreeing votes, the checker agreeing and no objection, the pretend result rounds to 92%. Five of six votes alone means 83% agreement—not 83% accuracy.

This mock does not change the original category, find missed contradictions, or measure whether someone is lying.
